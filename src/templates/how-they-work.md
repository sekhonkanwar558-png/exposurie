# How this person works

**This file is yours, and it is the one file here you are meant to keep
writing.** Everything else in `.exposurie/` was copied in once and left alone.
This one starts nearly empty on purpose, because its content cannot ship — it
is specific to one person and it does not exist yet.

## What it is for

The rules in `wiki-prompt.md` are somebody else's taste, shipped as a starting
point. They say what is generally worth recording. They cannot say what *this*
person considers signal, how they want to be written to, or which of the
defaults they would throw out.

Normally a brain acquires that slowly, by the person reading a bad page and
saying so. That takes months and most people never do it.

**But they have been correcting an agent all along**, in the transcripts this
brain is built from — telling it to stop explaining, to decide instead of ask,
to stop hedging, that it already got this wrong last week. **Those corrections
are taste, and they are already in the corpus.** Collect them here as you go,
and the brain gets a correction loop it was never given.

## What to record

Look for the person correcting *how the work is done*, not what the answer was:

- **Preferences about output** — length, hedging, formatting, tone. "Stop
  giving me options, just pick one." "Don't apologise, just fix it."
- **Preferences about process** — when to ask versus decide, how much to
  explain, whether to check in. "Tell me the mechanics before you argue about
  the design."
- **Repeated corrections.** The strongest signal in the corpus. The third time
  someone says the same thing, it is not a preference, it is a rule they are
  tired of restating.
- **What they call good and bad.** Direct verdicts on work — "this is exactly
  it", "this is the thing I keep telling you not to do" — with the thing they
  were reacting to.
- **What they refuse.** A standing no is worth more than three yeses, and it
  is the kind of thing that gets rediscovered painfully otherwise.

## What is not taste, and does not belong here

**A factual correction is not a preference.** "Wrong file", "that function
was renamed", "the date was the 14th" — those are facts, and they belong in
the brain's pages, not here. Recording them as taste produces a file that
says nothing and is expensive to read.

The test: would this change how you write *any* page, or does it only change
one fact on one page? Only the first belongs here.

Ordinary annoyance is not taste either. Someone frustrated at a bug is not
telling you anything about how they want to be worked with.

## How to write it

- **One line per rule, with the reason attached**, and the date it was first
  seen. A rule with no reason gets misapplied the first time it is inconvenient.
- **Quote them.** Their own sentence is the whole value; a paraphrase of a
  preference is a preference weakened.
- **Additive. Never delete.** If a preference is superseded, mark it superseded
  with the date and leave the old one visible — a change of mind is itself
  information about the person, and it may belong on their page.
- **Do not infer a rule from one offhand line.** Wait for it to repeat, or for
  it to be stated plainly. A brain that acts on a guess about someone's taste
  is worse than one that has no idea.

## How it is used

**Read this file before writing pages and before curating.** Where it
disagrees with `wiki-prompt.md` or `curate.md`, **this file wins** — it is
about the person whose brain this is, and those two are defaults from
somebody else.

Two things it cannot override, because they are what makes a brain trustworthy
rather than agreeable: **never invent a fact to satisfy a preference**, and
**never delete material because a preference makes it inconvenient.**
